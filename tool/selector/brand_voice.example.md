# Brand voice and selection criteria (example)

Copy this file to `brand_voice.md` in the same folder and rewrite it for your own
brand. Until you do, this neutral example is used. Claude reads it when picking
clips and writing headlines.

## Who is speaking
One or two lines: your name, what you do, who you help, the result you get them.

## Who is watching
The viewer you want: their situation, what they are stuck on, what they are tired of hearing.

## Phrases that are yours
Lines you say often and want clipped whenever they appear. One per bullet.
- (add your own)

## Proof you can point to
Specific numbers, results and time frames you are happy to see on screen.

## Voice rules
- Plain, direct sentences. No hype.
- No emoji.
- Spelling convention and currency you use.

## What makes a clip worth extracting
1. **Reframe**: takes a common belief and turns it round.
2. **Specific number with context**: never an abstract claim.
3. **Names a pain the viewer has lived**: in their words, not jargon.
4. **Teaches one step or principle** clearly enough to land in 30 to 90 seconds.
5. **Stands alone**: makes sense with no knowledge of the rest of the video.

## What disqualifies a moment
- Logistics ("let me share my screen", "can you hear me").
- Off-topic tangents.
- A thought that never resolves.
- Generic motivation with nothing specific in it.
- Heavy crosstalk or interruptions.

## Headline rules (the title at the top of the clip)
- Lowercase. No emoji, no quote marks, no exclamation marks.
- 3 to 7 words, hard cap. Longer headlines wrap badly.
- Aim for one clean line, or two balanced lines of at least three words each.
- A curiosity-loaded reframe or a specific stake.
- Avoid stock phrases such as "the secret to", "the truth about",
  "what nobody tells you", "this changes everything".
